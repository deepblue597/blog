Hello again! Today I would like to talk about a "hobby" I started experimenting with a few months ago. Self hosting. Before I start explaining what self hosting is, I would like to underline how important is how to have a basic understanding of how to self host some apps and how it can help you better grasp what costs you, both economically and on your privacy, when you use a third party service. After my experience, I have a better understanding of prons and cons when you are using a third party app to store your data and I can better evaluate if it is worth it or not.

## What is self hosting?

Self hosting is the practice of running and maintaining your own server to host applications, websites, or services instead of relying on third-party providers. This can be done using physical hardware that you own, or by renting virtual private servers (VPS) from cloud providers. Self hosting allows you to have full control over your data, privacy, and the software you use. Wether you want to store your photos, files, media or run applications that help you on your everyday life, a self hosting app can help you keep your data private and peronsliaze your experience on your needs.

## Why self host?

As mentioned earlier, self hosting gives you more control on what,where and how you are storing your data. In today's world, we take thousands if not millions of photos, which we don't look after the first few seconds we take them (or maybe after we upload them on instagram). This means that we usually pile up a lot of photos, and conveniently upload them on a cloud storage provider, wether this is apple, google or another app. Think about this for a second. You upload all your photos on a cloud provider, and stay there for ever. These photos maybe used for training an algorithm better detect your face, analyze the photos for better ads or even worse, be part of a data breach. Maybe you don't mind that much that an algorithm with your data is trained for a better personalized experience (you probably have seen those words written before), or you might not be afraid of a data breach. But you need to be informed about the potential risks, and you also need to know how you can store your data outside cloud, or at least encrypt them. That's why I suggest learning about self hosting. It is a good way to see what other options exist, learn about how precious your data is, understand risks and benefits on using cloud providers and make a informative decision.

## It seems hard

I may be biased on that, I come from a tech background which means I have a bit of understanding of how things work in the "software" world, but I truly believe that nowadays, most self hosting apps are plug and play. Yes, there is a learning curve in the beginning, when you start learning how to run these apps (on your pc, in a docker container etc) but after that, most of the apps you are going to need are pretty straight forward, have really good documentation and have a create community behind. Now I am not saying that you are not going to get stuck at some point or you might get a bit frustrated, but I think everything that is interesting to learn and conquer has some difficulty at first. Nothing valuable is gained easily. At the end of the day, LLMs like chatGPT and Mistral can really help you solve most of the issues you are going to have. Plus, you get a really nice feeling of accomplishment when you manage to run your self hosted app.

## But what about the cost

Well, you probably hear self hosting and thinking of big chunky pcs with lots of power and lots of storage that cost a lot of money. And yeah these pcs gice you the ability to host multiple apps and create pretty configurations with automations etc. But all you need to start is an old pc, laptop, a raspberry pi or even your current pc if you dont mind consuming some resources. I am running about 10 apps on my raspberry pi and I haven;t encountered any issues. Most of the things you will need especially in your early journey is just a laptop that is working.
